A Content Calendar That Actually Fits a Solo Creator's Week

Content calendars built for marketing teams assume you have someone drafting, someone approving, and someone scheduling. As a solo creator, you're all three people, which means most calendar templates are overbuilt for what you actually need.

Four statuses are enough: idea, drafted, scheduled, posted. You don't need a Kanban board with twelve columns. What you need is a fast way to see what's stuck — ideas that never became drafts, or drafts that never got scheduled — because that's where output actually breaks down.

Plan by platform and date together. The same idea might go out as a TikTok on Monday and a LinkedIn post on Wednesday. A calendar that only tracks dates, without which platform each entry belongs to, hides the fact that one platform is getting neglected.

Leave room for reactive content. If your whole calendar is booked out a month in advance, there's no room for the trending sound or the timely take that actually performs best. Plan your evergreen ideas ahead, and leave slots open for whatever's happening that week.

Review it weekly, not daily. Checking a content calendar every day creates anxiety without adding information. A ten-minute weekly review — what got posted, what's overdue, what needs an idea — is enough to keep it honest.
