🥡 Sunday Prep Ritual

Meal prep Sunday: prep once, eat well all week

One focused hour on Sunday buys you five easy weeknight dinners — here's exactly how to spend it.

🥗 📦 🍗 🍚
Why one hour changes everything

📦 Meal prep isn't about cooking 5 separate dinners

The version of meal prep that burns people out is trying to cook five entirely different, fully-finished meals in one afternoon. The version that actually works is simpler: prep a handful of flexible components — a protein, a grain, a couple of vegetables, one sauce — and reassemble them differently each night. Less like batch-cooking a restaurant menu, more like stocking a salad bar in your own fridge. 🥗

The formula

🎯 The "components, not meals" method

📦 The formula: 1 protein + 1 grain + 2 vegetables + 1 sauce, prepped separately, reassembled into a different combo each night.

This single shift is why meal prep sticks for some people and fails for others — components stay flexible and don't get boring by Wednesday the way five identical containers do.

The actual hour

⏱️ Your 60-minute Sunday prep, step by step

Roast a big protein (20 min)

Chicken thighs, tofu, or a batch of ground meat — season simply so it works across multiple flavor directions.

Cook one big batch of grain (20 min, mostly hands-off)

Rice, quinoa, or pasta — cook while the protein roasts, no extra active time needed.

Roast or chop two vegetables (15 min)

One roasted (broccoli, sweet potato) and one raw and crunchy (cucumber, cabbage) for texture variety through the week.

Make one all-purpose sauce (5 min)

A vinaigrette, a yogurt sauce, or a soy-based dressing — this is what keeps the same components from tasting repetitive.

Reassemble it

🍽️ 5 completely different dinners from the same prep

Monday: Grain Bowl 🥗

Grain + protein + veg + sauce, straight up

Tuesday: Wrap 🌯

Same components rolled in a tortilla with a little cheese

Wednesday: Stir-Fry 🍳

Reheat protein and veg together in a hot pan with soy sauce

Thursday: Soup 🍲

Add broth to the grain, protein, and veg for an instant soup

Friday: Loaded Salad 🥬

Fresh greens + all the prepped components + sauce

Quick answers

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is component-based meal prep really faster than making full meals?

Yes — cooking a big batch of one protein, one grain, and two vegetables takes less active time than cooking five separate, fully-finished dishes, and gives you far more variety.

How do I keep prepped meals from tasting repetitive by Thursday?

Change the sauce or format (bowl, wrap, stir-fry, soup) rather than the ingredients themselves — that's usually enough to make it feel like a different meal.

Can I meal prep if I only have an hour a week?

Yes — that's exactly the timeframe this method is built around. Roasting protein and vegetables together and cooking grain alongside keeps everything mostly hands-off.

Should I portion meals into containers or keep components separate?

Keeping components separate until you're ready to eat gives more flexibility and prevents things like crunchy vegetables from going soggy sitting in a sauce for days.

🎉 Final thoughts

You don't need seven identical containers lined up in your fridge to call it meal prep. One protein, one grain, two vegetables, and one sauce — reassembled differently each night — is enough to make weeknights genuinely easy. 📦🍽️
