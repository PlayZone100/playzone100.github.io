A sketch is the oldest portrait style there is, and it is having a moment again. It is black and white, so it hides messy backgrounds and bad color. It looks serious and personal, so it makes strong gifts, memorial portraits and profile pictures. And it prints cleanly on almost any paper.

This guide explains the difference between pencil and charcoal, which photos work best, the free workflow, how to print the result and how to fix common problems. If you like painted looks instead, read our photo to painting guide or the full Photo Trend Studio review.

1. Why sketch styles work so well

A sketch removes color, so everything depends on light, shadow and line. That makes the AI focus on the shape of the face, the eyes and the edges of fur or clothing. When the original photo has clear contrast, the result looks like a real drawing. When the original is flat, the sketch looks empty. So the photo matters more here than in almost any other style.

3. The best photos for a sketch

Clear light and shadow

Side light from a window gives the face shape. Flat, even light on a cloudy day gives a faint sketch.

A sharp subject

Fine lines need sharp edges. Use your sharpest frame, especially around eyes and fur.

A big face or clear subject

Crop closer before uploading. A small face in a wide shot loses its features in black and white.

A simple background

A plain wall, a blurred garden or a sky keeps the focus on the subject. Busy scenes turn into scribbles, especially in charcoal.

Visible eyes

Eyes carry the likeness. Sunglasses and deep shadows over the eyes give generic results.

6. Printing your sketch

Sketches print well because black and white is forgiving. Start from the highest-resolution original and download the result at the best quality available. Ask your print shop for the recommended size at your file's resolution. Matte or slightly textured paper makes pencil look more real, and heavier paper suits charcoal.
