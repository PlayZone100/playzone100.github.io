1. Why the photo matters most

An AI style tool reads shapes, edges and color areas. It finds the face, the hair, the clothes and the background, then redraws each one in the chosen look. Every weakness in the photo carries through: noise becomes grain, shadows become dark blobs, small faces lose their features.

The good news is that photo problems are easy to fix. You change where you stand, where the light comes from and how close you are. None of it costs money. If you want the style side as well, start with our AI photo trends overview and the Photo Trend Studio review.

2. Light: the biggest single factor

Use window light or open shade

Stand facing a window, or step into the shade of a building outdoors. The light is large and soft, so it wraps around the face. Cartoon styles then have clear shapes to outline, and painting styles have gentle gradients to turn into brushwork.

Avoid harsh and mixed light

Midday sun creates deep eye shadows and bright patches. A phone flash flattens the face and creates a shiny forehead. Mixed light, such as a warm lamp plus a blue window, tints skin in odd colors that the style then exaggerates.

Side light for sketch and charcoal

Black-and-white styles need contrast. Light coming from one side gives the face dimension, which makes pencil and charcoal look like real drawings. See our photo to sketch guide.

Golden hour is a bonus

The hour after sunrise or before sunset gives warm, soft light that flatters almost every style, especially watercolor and oil painting.

3. Framing and distance

With a small face in a wide shot, the AI has few pixels to work with, so features become guesses. Move closer or crop before uploading. For portraits, head and shoulders is the sweet spot. For avatars, leave a little room around the head for round crops.

Shoot at eye level. Holding the phone high makes the forehead large and the chin small. Holding it low does the opposite. Both distortions get exaggerated by cartoon and 3D styles. For selfies, hold the phone at arm's length rather than very close, because close wide-angle shots stretch the nose and forehead.

4. Sharpness and resolution

Tap the screen on the eyes before you shoot so the camera focuses where it matters. Hold still or brace your elbows. Indoors, light matters more than megapixels, because a dim room makes the phone slow its shutter and blur the shot.

Always upload the original file from your camera roll, not a screenshot or a photo sent through a chat app, which compresses and shrinks it. If you plan to print the result, the highest-resolution original is essential. Our gift ideas guide has print tips.

5. Backgrounds

A plain wall, a blurred garden or a clear sky keeps the focus on the subject. Busy backgrounds with shelves, signs and crowds turn into scattered shapes, especially in pixel art, charcoal and manga. If you cannot change the background, step forward, away from the wall behind you, so the camera blurs it.

Avoid text in the frame. Signs and shirts with writing are often redrawn as garbled letters.

6. Faces, eyes and expressions

Eyes carry most of the likeness. Keep them open, lit and unobstructed. Sunglasses, hair across the eyes and deep brow shadows lead to generic results. A relaxed, natural expression works best, because cartoon and anime styles exaggerate whatever is there, so a big open-mouth laugh becomes extreme.

Look at the lens, or just past it. A slight head turn of 10 to 20 degrees adds dimension, while a steep angle changes the face shape. Glasses are fine if the lenses do not reflect a window. Hats are fine if the brim does not shadow the eyes. For platform-specific avatars, read the AI profile picture guide.

7. Pets, kids, couples and groups

Pets

Get down to the animal's eye level and photograph near a window. Use a toy or treat to hold attention, and take many shots because pets move. Dark fur needs light on the face so the eyes and nose are visible. See the AI pet portrait guide.

Kids

Shoot candidly in daylight and do not ask for a posed smile. Natural expressions look better after styling.

Couples

Keep both faces at the same height and the same distance from the camera, evenly lit, with a little space between heads. Our couple portrait guide goes deeper.

Groups

Results get worse with every extra face because each one gets fewer pixels. For groups of more than three, step back, keep everyone in one row and consider styling smaller groups separately.

9. The 30-second checklist

  1. Is the light soft and coming from the front or side?
  2. Is the subject sharp, especially the eyes?
  3. Does the subject fill at least half the frame?
  4. Is the camera at eye level?
  5. Is the background plain and free of text?
  6. Is it the original file, not a screenshot?

Frequently asked questions

What is the best photo for AI art?

A sharp, well-lit photo where the subject fills most of the frame, the eyes are visible and the background is simple.

Does the photo resolution matter for AI styles?

Yes. A sharp, high-resolution original gives cleaner edges and is essential if you plan to print the result.

Can I use an old or scanned photo?

Yes, if the face is clear and not badly blurred. Try the cleanest scan you have.

Should I smile or keep a neutral face?

A relaxed, natural expression works best. Extreme expressions can be exaggerated by cartoon styles.

Do selfies work?

Yes. Hold the phone at eye level, use soft light and avoid wide-angle distortion by not holding it too close.
