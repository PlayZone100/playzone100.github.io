How to Negotiate a Brand Deal Without Losing the Offer

Most creators either accept the first number a brand sends, or ask for more and immediately worry they blew the whole deal. Negotiation isn't confrontation — it's just making sure the number matches the work.

Never counter without a reason. "Can we do more?" is easy to ignore. "My average views on this format are 40% higher than what we discussed, so I'd suggest $X" gives the brand something concrete to take back to their budget owner. Numbers move conversations; vibes don't.

Separate the fee from the extras. Usage rights, whitelisting, exclusivity and usage duration are not part of your base rate — they're add-ons. Bundling them into one number makes it look like you're asking for more than you are, when really you're asking for the right things to be paid for.

Respond within 24-48 hours. Brands are often talking to several creators at once. A fast, clear reply — even just "got it, reviewing and will confirm by Thursday" — keeps you in the conversation while you think it through.

Know your walk-away number before you start. Decide privately what the lowest acceptable rate is before you're mid-conversation with a brand manager. Negotiating in real time without a floor is how creators end up agreeing to something they regret.

A range beats a single number. Opening with a range like "$900–$1,400 depending on usage rights" gives the brand room to land somewhere fair without either side feeling like they lost the negotiation.
