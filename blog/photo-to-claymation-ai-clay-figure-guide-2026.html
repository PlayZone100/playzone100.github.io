Claymation is the look of hand-made stop-motion films: soft rounded shapes, a matte surface, tiny fingerprint-like bumps and warm, friendly color. It is one of the cutest AI styles, and it makes ordinary photos feel handmade and charming.

This guide explains what makes the style work, which photos suit it, a free workflow, ideas for using the result and fixes for common problems. For more styles, see the Photo Trend Studio review.

1. What makes the claymation look

Four ingredients define it. Round shapes: noses, cheeks and fingers become soft blobs. Matte texture: surfaces look hand-sculpted, with tiny bumps. Simple color areas: hair, skin and clothes become clean blocks of color. Big friendly eyes: expression is simple and readable. The AI applies these on top of your photo, so simple photos give the best figures.

2. The best photos for claymation

A big subject

Crop closer. A clay figure needs a clear head and shoulders, not a tiny person in a wide scene.

Simple shapes

Round faces, short hair and bold clothing turn into clay beautifully. Very fine details, like thin hair strands and small jewelry, get smoothed away.

Clear, soft light

Even light shows the shape of the face without harsh shadows, which clay styles treat as dark patches.

A plain background

A plain wall or sky lets the figure stand out. Busy backgrounds become lumpy shapes.

Pets and kids

Round faces and clear markings are ideal. See our AI pet portrait guide.
