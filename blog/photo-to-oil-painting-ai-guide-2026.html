Three myths I believed

"Any photo will work if the filter is good enough."

No. Our first test was a flat, grey indoor snapshot. The result looked like the same photo with a texture sticker on it. Oil painting is a lighting style. If the photo has no light to interpret, there is nothing to paint.

"More detail in the photo means more detail in the painting."

Often the opposite. Fine detail like leaves, text and busy crowds turns into brushy noise. A face with a calm background kept its character; a market scene lost it.

"It will look the same on every subject."

Not at all. Pets and flowers came out surprisingly rich. Group photos were the weakest. Pick the subject first and the style second.

Mistakes to skip

  • Starting from a low-resolution screenshot. Brush texture cannot rescue a blurry source.
  • Harsh midday flash. It flattens the face and the painting looks plastic.
  • Judging at full zoom only. Always check at the size people will actually see it.
  • Expecting a bold, graphic look. For that, pop art is the better pick. For softer, see watercolor.

Printing on canvas
