Freelance Proposal Template: Structure, Example and Tips (2026)

Updated September 20, 2026 · 11-minute read

Most freelancers do good work. The ones who get paid well also send good proposals. A freelance proposal is the document that turns "I am interested" into "yes, let's start," and a weak one can lose you a project you were perfectly qualified for.

Below is a proposal template you can adapt right away: the structure, sample wording for every section, and the details that separate a proposal that gets ignored from one that gets signed. What makes a freelance proposal different?

Corporate proposals can run to dozens of pages with legal boilerplate. Freelance proposals should be shorter, warmer and faster to read. The person deciding is often the business owner or a marketing manager who is busy and not an expert in your field. They want to know four things: do you understand my problem, what exactly will I get, how long will it take, and what will it cost.

A good freelance proposal is usually two to five pages, written in plain English, with a clean layout and a direct next step. It also sets the stage for the agreement that follows; our freelance contract template guide shows what to include once the client says yes.

The freelance proposal template, section by section

Copy the structure below, then replace the wording in brackets with details from your own conversation with the client.

Section 1: Header and short intro

Proposal for [Client Company]
Prepared by [Your Name] · [Date] · Valid until [Date]

Hi [First name], thank you for talking with me about [project]. Below is my plan for [main goal], including scope, timeline and investment.

Keep this short. The header confirms that the document is personal to them, and the validity date sets a gentle deadline.

Section 2: Summary of what you understood

You want to [goal, in their words]. Right now [current problem or obstacle], which is causing [consequence: lost leads, wasted time, slow sales]. Success for this project means [measurable outcome or clear result].

This is where you win trust. Use the phrases the client used on your call. If they said "our website feels dated," write "feels dated," not "requires a modern redesign." It proves you listened.

Section 3: Your proposed solution

I recommend [approach]. This will involve: [step 1], [step 2] and [step 3]. I chose this approach because [reason that connects to their goal].

Explain the "why," not only the "what." Clients hire freelancers for judgment as much as for skills. One sentence of reasoning shows expertise better than a page of technical terms.

Section 4: Deliverables and what is not included

Included: [item, quantity, format], [item, quantity, format], [number] rounds of revisions.
Not included: [items you will not do, such as copywriting, hosting, ongoing support].

Exclusions protect you from scope creep and protect the client from surprises. For a detailed approach, see our guide on the scope of work template.

Section 5: Timeline

Week 1: [discovery and planning]
Week 2: [first draft or prototype]
Week 3: [revisions and feedback]
Week 4: [final delivery]
This timeline assumes I receive [materials] by [date].

The last sentence matters. It records that the client's delays can move your deadline, which prevents blame later.

Section 6: Investment

Option A: [Basic package] — [price]
Option B: [Recommended package] — [price]
Option C: [Premium package] — [price]
Payment: [50%] to start, [50%] on delivery. Prices are in [currency] and valid until [date].

Offering two or three options is one of the most effective tactics in freelance sales. Instead of deciding whether to hire you, the client decides which package fits, and mid-tier options often become the most popular. If your client is abroad, state the currency and payment method clearly, as explained in our guide to invoicing international clients.

Section 7: Proof

Recently I helped [similar client] achieve [result] by [what you did]. "[Short testimonial]" — [Name, role]. You can see more at [link].

Choose the example closest to this client's industry or problem. Two sentences and a real result are more persuasive than a page of biography. If you are new and have few clients, use a personal project, a volunteer job or a clear before-and-after sample of your process.

Section 8: Next steps

To go ahead, reply "approved" to this email and I will send a short agreement and the first invoice. I can start on [date]. If you have questions, we can talk any time this week.

Make the action one easy step. Every extra step you ask for (print, sign, scan, upload) reduces the number of people who complete it.

A short example: web designer proposal

Here is how the first sections might look for a realistic project:

Summary. You sell handmade ceramics online and want more customers to finish checkout. Right now your product pages load slowly on phones and shipping costs appear only at the last step, which is likely causing abandoned carts. Success means a faster, clearer store that lets customers see the full price early.

Solution. I will redesign the product and cart pages, compress images, and show shipping estimates on each product. I recommend this order because the biggest drop-off appears to happen at the cart, so fixing it first delivers results sooner.

Investment. Standard package: $1,800. Standard plus one month of support: $2,300.

Notice how each part refers to the client's situation, gives a reason, and ends with a clear number. That is all a good proposal needs to do.

How to price a freelance proposal

Hourly, fixed or value-based?

Hourly pricing is simple but rewards slowness and makes clients watch the clock. Fixed pricing gives the client certainty and rewards your efficiency, as long as scope is well defined. Value-based pricing ties the fee to the result the client gets and works best when the outcome is measurable. Many freelancers estimate hours internally, then quote a fixed price with a buffer.

Build in a buffer

Include time for feedback, small changes and communication. Most projects take longer than the pure working time, and unpaid overrun is a hidden cost that quietly lowers your real hourly rate.

Ask for a deposit

A deposit filters serious clients and covers your early time. Many freelancers ask for a portion upfront and the rest on milestones or delivery. When you deliver, bill promptly with a clear invoice, as described in our article on how to create a professional invoice.

Freelance proposal mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with your life story. Start with the client's problem.
  • Using industry jargon. Clients do not need your vocabulary; they need clarity.
  • Sending only a price. A bare number invites comparison with the cheapest bidder.
  • Overpromising. Never guarantee rankings, sales or followers you cannot control.
  • Leaving out timeline or exclusions. Ambiguity now becomes conflict later.
  • Not following up. Send a polite reminder after two to four business days.
  • Bad presentation. Inconsistent fonts, missing logos or typos suggest careless work.

Pre-send checklist

  • Client name, company and project title are correct
  • Problem is described in the client's own words
  • Solution explains the reason, not just the task
  • Deliverables and exclusions are listed
  • Timeline includes what you need from the client
  • Price, currency, payment terms and validity date are clear
  • One relevant proof point is included
  • Next step is a single easy action
  • Saved as a clean PDF and proofread once more

Frequently asked questions

What should a freelance proposal include?

A freelance proposal should include a summary, the client's problem, your solution, deliverables, timeline, pricing, proof of your experience and a clear next step.

Is it okay to use the same proposal template for every client?

Reuse the structure and design, but rewrite the problem, solution, scope and price for each client so it feels specific to them.

Should freelancers charge for writing proposals?

Most freelancers do not charge for standard proposals, but very large or research-heavy proposals may justify a paid discovery phase.

What file format is best for sending a proposal?

A PDF is the most common choice because it keeps formatting intact on every device. Some freelancers also send a web link with an accept button.

Final thoughts

A proposal is a sales conversation on paper. Show that you listened, explain your plan simply, price it clearly and make the next step easy. Use the template above as your starting structure, personalise the parts that matter, and follow up. Do that consistently and your win rate will climb.
