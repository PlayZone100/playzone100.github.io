First things first

💧 Before food, there's water (and yes, this matters)

Last night's choices happened, and now here you are, squinting at sunlight like it personally wronged you. 😵 Before you even think about food, get fluids in — water, an electrolyte drink, or coconut water if you have it. Alcohol is dehydrating, and a lot of "hangover fog" is at least partly your body asking for water it didn't get. Then, once you can stomach it, food actually does help.

The formula

🥑 What actually helps (and why)

There's no magic cure, but some fridge staples genuinely help more than others — replacing electrolytes, settling your stomach, and giving your body easy fuel.

🎯 The formula: something salty (broth, crackers, eggs) + something with potassium (banana, avocado, coconut water) + carbs that are gentle on the stomach (toast, rice, plain crackers) = the closest thing to a real fix.

☕Too much caffeine
🌶️Very spicy or heavy food

Quick answers

❓ Frequently asked questions

Should I eat if I feel nauseous?

Start small — plain crackers, toast, or a few sips of broth. Forcing a big meal on a nauseous stomach usually backfires; ease in gently instead.

Is coffee a good idea for a hangover?

A small amount is usually fine, but too much can worsen dehydration and jitteriness. Prioritize water first.

What actually causes hangover symptoms?

A combination of dehydration, low blood sugar, inflammation, and disrupted sleep — which is why fluids, gentle food, and rest all help, rather than any single "cure."

When should I be concerned rather than just riding it out?

If you're unable to keep any fluids down, feel severely dizzy or confused, or symptoms last more than 24 hours, it's worth checking in with a doctor rather than waiting it out.

🎉 Final thoughts

There's no miracle cure, but water first, gentle salty-and-potassium-rich food second, and genuine rest third will get you back to functional faster than powering through on willpower alone. 💧🥑
