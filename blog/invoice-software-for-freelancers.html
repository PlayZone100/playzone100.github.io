What freelancers need vs. what enterprise tools sell

Most invoicing platforms are built for the widest possible market, which means they're priced and featured for small accounts teams: multi-user permissions, approval workflows, integrations with a dozen accounting platforms. A solo freelancer sending 4-10 invoices a month needs almost none of that.

What actually matters at freelance scale is simpler: a clean invoice number, correct tax handling, a currency your client recognizes, and a PDF that looks professional. Invoice Studio is built around that narrower, more common need — one file, no account, no per-seat pricing you'll never use.

Real invoice line items, by role

A good freelance invoice describes the work in words a client's finance team recognizes instantly. A few examples:

  • Web developer: "Marketing site, 5 pages, responsive build" (fixed price), or "Development, 24 hours at $60" (hourly)
  • Graphic designer: "Brand identity: logo, colour palette, typography" as one line, "Revisions, additional round" as a separate line if requested
  • Freelance writer: "Blog articles, 4 × $90" or "Long-form guide, 2,500 words at $0.25/word"
  • Consultant: "Strategy workshop, 1 day" and "Follow-up report", with expenses on their own line
  • Translator: "Translation, EN→ES, 3,200 words at $0.08"

Fixed price or hourly, the rule holds: one clear line per deliverable, a quantity, and a rate. Vague lines like "services rendered" are the ones clients query and delay paying.
