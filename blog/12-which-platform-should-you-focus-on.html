Which Platform Should You Actually Focus On?

Trying to be great on eight platforms at once is how most creators end up mediocre on all of them. The creators who grow fastest usually pick one primary platform and treat the rest as distribution, not creation.

Pick based on format, not trend. If you think in short spoken hooks, video-first platforms reward that instantly. If you think in structured arguments, long-form or written platforms fit better. Chasing whatever platform is trending this quarter ignores what you're actually good at making.

Look at where your best-performing content already lives. Before choosing a "primary" platform, check which existing post — on any platform — got disproportionate engagement relative to your following. That's a signal about what your audience actually wants from you, not just where you happened to post.

Primary doesn't mean exclusive. A primary platform is where you create first and invest the most editing time. Everything else can be a repurposed, shorter version of that same idea — a byproduct of the main work, not a second full-time job.

Reassess every few months, not every week. Platform algorithms shift, and it's tempting to chase the shift. But switching your primary platform every few weeks resets your audience-building progress each time. Give a platform a real quarter before deciding it's not working.

Track performance per platform, not just overall. Follower count on its own hides which platform is actually converting into views, engagement, or income. Splitting your numbers out makes the decision obvious instead of a guess.
