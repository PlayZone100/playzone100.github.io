You Probably Have Too Many Creator Apps Open

A scheduling app, a rate calculator, a spreadsheet for income, a separate editor, a third-party media kit site, a link-in-bio tool — each one solved a single problem well, and together they're a part-time job just to keep synced.

Every extra tool is another login, another price, another place data can go stale. When your calendar lives in one app and your income lives in another, "how am I actually doing this month" requires opening three tabs and doing math by hand, which means most creators just... don't check.

Subscriptions add up faster than they feel like they do. Five tools at $10-15/month each is $50-75/month, every month, whether you post daily or not at all that month. For a solo creator, that's a real chunk of a single brand deal, recurring forever.

Switching costs are real, but so is the ongoing cost of staying scattered. It's normal to hesitate before consolidating — exporting data, learning a new interface. But weigh that one-time cost against months or years of monthly fees and context-switching between five different logins.

Look for tools that cover adjacent tasks, not just one. A calendar tool that also tracks status from idea to posted saves a second app. A rate calculator that also logs the deal saves a spreadsheet. Consolidation isn't about one mega-tool doing everything perfectly — it's about fewer places your workflow can break.

Data portability matters more than feature count. Before consolidating into anything, check that you can export your data in a plain format (CSV, JSON) you actually own. A tool with more features but no export is a worse trap than the five separate tools you started with.
